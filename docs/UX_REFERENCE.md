# UX Reference — Tools That Enable or Enhance Artistic Output

> *Created 2026-08-06.* Companion to `POSITIONING.md` §5 (the landscape) and
> §7 (design consequences). Where POSITIONING proves the *position is empty*,
> this document captures **what the closest tools actually do with their UX** —
> so PoesIA's interface choices are grounded in observed practice, not
> invention. Same honesty rule as POSITIONING: claims are tagged
> `[verified]` (read directly in this session) or `[known]` (widely
> documented; primary page live but JS-rendered).

## 1. The closest cousin: Verse by Verse (Google, 2020)

- Human writes **each line**; the machine suggests continuations in the
  voice of classic poets (Dickinson, Whitman, Poe, …). The human is the
  author; the AI holds options. `[known]`
- Sunset experiment — the POSITIONING finding still holds: *no validation,
  no teaching-*why*, no aftercare, no library.* `[verified]` (POSITIONING §5)
- **UX lesson for PoesIA:** the human-writes-first interaction is proven
  viable; its failure was *aftercare* — nothing taught, nothing kept.
  PoesIA's teaching voice + memoria are exactly the missing layers.

## 2. PoemPortraits (Es Devlin + Ross Goodwin, Google Arts & Culture, 2019)

- **Interaction:** a visitor donates **one word**; the machine composes a
  poem around it. Every poem joins a *collective poem*. Tagline verified
  this session: *"Es Devlin invites you to take part in a collective poem —
  woven at the intersection of AI and human collaboration."* `[verified]`
- It is **installation art, not an instrument**: the human gives one word,
  the machine holds the pen. `[verified]` (POSITIONING §5)
- **UX lesson for PoesIA:** the *invitation framing* matters — a person is
  invited into collaboration, not asked to run a generator. But the power
  balance is the anti-pattern for PoesIA: one word is not authorship.
  PoesIA keeps the human's hands on every line.

## 3. Sudowrite (AI for professional fiction writers)

- Feature taxonomy (verified this session from `docs.sudowrite.com`):
  `Write`, `Rewrite`, `Describe`, `Brainstorm`, `First Draft`, `Expand`,
  `Canvas`, `Visualize`, `Quick Edit`, `Selection Menu`, `Find and Replace`,
  `Story Bible`, `Braindump`, `Muse`, `Feedback`. `[verified]`
- **UX lessons for PoesIA:**
  - **Verb-first feature names** (`Write`, `Rewrite`, `Expand`) — each tool
    is a *gesture the human performs*, not a model the human invokes. PoesIA
    should name flows as verbs/experiences, not internals
    ("scan", "workshop", "keep" — not "generate candidates").
  - **The human stays in a rich editing surface** — a document canvas, not a
    prompt box. PoesIA's interactive selector is the right instinct; the
    canvas is worth considering as it grows.
  - **Credits/plans are explicit** — production tooling for professionals.
    PoesIA is deliberately the opposite: private, accumulative, free-form.
  - Production-oriented prose for novelists — the POSITIONING gap holds:
    *built for professional output, not for expression.* `[verified]`

## 4. NovelAI (AI anime art + storytelling)

- Tagline verified this session: *"AI Anime Image Generator & Storyteller …
  a suite of tools to easily bring your imagination to life without
  limits."* Emphasizes a **"Purpose-built Creative Editor"** — *"an
  interface tailored towards making generating imagery simple and fast …
  keeps you focused on the thing that matters most, your imagination."*
  `[verified]`
- **UX lesson for PoesIA:** the interface must keep the human *focused on
  their own imagination* — the tool should disappear behind the act of
  making. Every line of CLI output that talks about `metre=`/`theme=` pulls
  attention back to the machinery (the exact failure the 2026-08-06 UX
  re-voice fixed).

## 5. Suno (AI music for everyone)

- Tagline verified this session: *"Music doesn't stop. Neither do we. Suno
  is a music company built to amplify imagination. Where ideas flow into
  sound and songs become yours."* And: *"we've focused on delivering an
  extraordinary creative experience for anyone who wants to make music —
  whether it's their first time or their thousandth."* `[verified]`
- **UX lessons for PoesIA:**
  - **"Songs become yours"** — the output is framed as belonging to the
    human, not generated by the machine. Matches PoesIA's
    *draft/proposal/scaffold* language discipline exactly.
  - **Every level welcome** — "first time or thousandth" is the same
    audience POSITIONING §4 claims (no prior poetry knowledge required).
    The tool must not assume craft vocabulary.

## 6. Artbreeder (generative image breeding)

- Bare marketing page (verified this session); core interaction `[known]`:
  users **breed** images by blending and adjusting — a continuous, iterative
  dialogue, not one-shot generation.
- **UX lesson for PoesIA:** *iterative refinement as the default loop* —
  the human keeps nudging toward what they want. PoesIA's
  generate → scan → repair → keep loop is the poetic analogue; the UX should
  make the iteration feel like *breeding a line*, not *fixing a bug*.

## 7. What this means for PoesIA (design principles)

Synthesized from the above, mapped to the four movements:

1. **Invite, don't operate.** (PoemPortraits) — the tool opens with an
   invitation ("what are you carrying?"), not a parameter list.
2. **The human's hands stay on the work.** (Verse by Verse) — human-writes-
   first is the default; machine output is *option*, never *finished work*.
3. **Name the gesture, not the mechanism.** (Sudowrite) — verbs and felt
   experiences (`scan`, `workshop`, `keep`), never "candidate generator"
   or "scoring mode".
4. **The interface disappears behind the imagination.** (NovelAI) — no raw
   scores, no backend telemetry in the default path (implemented:
   `--verbose` gate).
5. **The output belongs to the human.** (Suno) — "songs become yours";
   PoesIA: the draft is scaffolding, the poem is what you keep (implemented).
6. **Every level is welcome.** (Suno) — plain language from the first line
   (implemented: teaching voice + humanized selector).
7. **Iteration is the product.** (Artbreeder) — the loop is breeding a line,
   not debugging. The teaching voice *is* the iteration feedback.
