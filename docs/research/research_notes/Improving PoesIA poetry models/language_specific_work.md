# Automatic poetry generation in Spanish, Italian, German, French and English since 2018, and multilingual poetry models (as of 16 October 2026)

Scope note: this file avoids repeating `poesia-calaveritas/docs/research/research_notes/PoesIA shared poem quality layer/metre_and_rhyme.md`. That file already covers the scansion tools (Rantanplan, Jumper, ALBERTI as a scanner, Haider's tagger), the Llama-2 zero-shot syllable numbers, PoeLM's filter statistics, Van de Cruys's reverse decoding, Ou et al.'s lyric translation, the Greek generate–verify–refine loop, and Anthropic's rhyme-planning result. Those are cited here only where the per-language map needs them. Numbers here were read from the primary paper text (PDF or HTML) unless marked otherwise.

## Spanish: PoeLM, ALBERTI/PULPO, sonnet corpora (DISCO, ADSO), constrained GPT-2, recent LLM work

### Takeaway
Only two Spanish *generation* systems with measured results turned up. **PoeLM** (Ormazabal et al. 2022) is a 760M LM trained from scratch on 700 GB of Spanish mC4 prose with structure codes, plus heavy sampling and filtering. **The Tiny Poet** (Stoia et al. 2025) is a 117M Spanish GPT-2 fine-tuned on 5,262 poems, with backtracking constraints. Both reach form correctness only through an external, deterministic metre/rhyme check. Both report the remaining weakness as meaning and cohesion, not form. ALBERTI/PULPO is an *analysis* model, not a generator. I found no peer-reviewed 2023–2026 study that measures LLM metre on Spanish sonnets or endecasílabos.

### Cited Findings
- **PoeLM, model and data:** 760M-parameter transformer for Spanish, trained over 100B tokens (stopped at about 85B) on a 700 GB subset of mC4. The Basque model is 350M on EusCrawl (2.5 GB). Training uses no poetry: prose phrases get a "structure descriptor" prefix (syllables per line plus line-final rhyme sound). At inference the scheme's rhyme sounds are sampled from the five most common in the corpus, and **k = 3,000** candidates are generated per poem. Evaluation unit: the first stanza (4 × 11 syllables) of Spanish sonnets. Perplexity was measured on the DISCO sonnet set. — [Ormazabal et al., Findings EMNLP 2022, arXiv:2205.12206, §3–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.12206)
- **PoeLM, results:** 30.9% of Spanish candidates pass all filters (baseline LM 0%). In human ranking against stanzas by renowned poets and by laymen, PoeLM's stanza was preferred over the expert poem in 37.3% of pairs and over the layman poem in 39.3%, rising to 42.7% with reranking by unconditioned fluency. PoeLM was ranked first among the three in 19.3% of cases. — [arXiv:2205.12206, Tables 1, 3–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.12206)
- **The Tiny Poet (Stoia, Ureña-López, Montejo-Ráez 2025):** base model PlanTL-GOB-ES/gpt2-base-bne (117M parameters, 12 layers, pretrained on 570 GB from the Biblioteca Nacional de España, byte-level BPE with 50,262 vocabulary). It was fine-tuned on 5,262 poems (772,746 tokens) by 109 authors from 15 Spanish-speaking countries, taken from the Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes. Rhyme, metre and stanza form are enforced at decoding with a verse stack and **backtracking**. A rhyme-search heuristic cut sonnet generation time from 40–90 minutes to about 3 minutes. — [Procesamiento del Lenguaje Natural 74 (2025) 321–333, doi:10.26342/2025-74-22, open-access PDF](http://journal.sepln.org/sepln/ojs/ojs/index.php/pln/article/download/6684/4075)
- **The Tiny Poet, evaluation:** 21 generated poems were mixed with 20 corpus poems in a Turing test: 20 participants, 820 labels. 143 labels were "machine poem judged human". Precision of "real" judgements was 66.58% (33.42% error). Cohen's κ between annotators was 0.2153. Six generated poems were judged real by majority vote. In expert Likert ratings, generated poems scored lower than real ones, most of all on "use of language" and "evokes mental images". The authors say the system "generates valid poetic texts, but which may lack of cohesion and meaning." — [doi:10.26342/2025-74-22, §5](http://journal.sepln.org/sepln/ojs/ojs/index.php/pln/article/download/6684/4075)
- **The Tiny Poet authors' observation on ChatGPT (anecdotal, one prompt each):** a GPT-3.5 Turbo "soneto" on spring had "many [lines] not hendecasyllabic" and did not follow ABBA ABBA CDC DCD. GPT-4's answer was "even worse" (free-length lines). — [same paper, §2](http://journal.sepln.org/sepln/ojs/ojs/index.php/pln/article/download/6684/4075)
- **ALBERTI / PULPO:** ALBERTI further pretrains multilingual BERT on "over 12 million verses from 12 languages" (the PULPO corpus). It is evaluated on Spanish stanza-type classification and on metrical-pattern prediction for Spanish, English and German, with state of the art reported for German against rule-based systems. It does not generate. — [de la Rosa et al., PLN 71 (2023), arXiv:2307.01387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01387). A search snippet gave PULPO as "over 72 million words" (unverified, not seen in the paper). The 2026 JAIR survey describes ALBERTI as "trained on the PULPO corpus containing poems in 12 languages". — [Koziev & Sinev 2026, doi:10.1613/jair.1.20584](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/20584/27307/55382)
- **Spanish corpora listed by the 2026 survey:** a Spanish poetry dataset of 5,133 poems with metadata (Garzón & Pérez 2020, HF `andreamorgar/spanish_poetry`). **DISCO PAL**, the Diachronic Spanish Sonnet Corpus with psychological and affective labels, 4,530 sonnets (Barbado et al. 2022, LaTeCH). Navarro et al. (2016, LREC) is the metrically annotated Golden Age sonnet corpus (ADSO). — [Koziev & Sinev 2026, appendix datasets](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/20584/27307/55382)
- **Literature share:** in the survey's LLM-assisted count of ACL Anthology poetry papers 2006–2025, Spanish has 1 entry, against Chinese 27, English 18, French 6, Basque 3 and German 1. Italian is not listed. This is a rough automatic classification by the authors. — [Koziev & Sinev 2026, Table 7](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/20584/27307/55382)
- **Survey inconsistency (flag):** the survey says "Tian and Peng (2022) proposed a method for fine-tuning LMs to generate Spanish syllabic poetry." Tian & Peng (NAACL 2022) is the English zero-shot sonnet system Zest (see English section). This looks like a survey error. — [Koziev & Sinev 2026, §4.2](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/20584/27307/55382); [Tian & Peng 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.naacl-main.262)
- Earlier Spanish work cited by the survey: PoeTryMe adapted to Spanish with templates (Gonçalo Oliveira, Hervás et al. 2014). A multilingual (English, Spanish, Portuguese) poetry assistant (Gonçalo Oliveira, Mendes et al. 2017). — [Koziev & Sinev 2026](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/20584/27307/55382)

### Inferences
- The Spanish evidence repeats PoesIA's own architecture. A generic Spanish LM plus a deterministic syllable/rhyme engine yields form-correct verse, and the measured loss is in coherence and imagery. Better form engines will not lift quality further. What lifts it is meaning-aware selection (PoeLM's fluency rerank gained 3.4 points of preference) and better content.
- PoeLM's 30.9% pass rate came from a purpose-trained model with explicit per-line codes and 3,000 samples. An instruction-tuned 8B model without codes should be expected to pass far less often per sample (the Llama-2 English rates in the prior note are 0.8–27% per line). So per-line repair matters more than raw sampling.
- No Spanish work targets Mexican modernismo or romanticism specifically. DISCO is the nearest diachronic sonnet resource. Whether it covers Latin American authors well was not checked.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 peer-reviewed measurement of GPT-4-class or open-weight LLM metre/rhyme success on Spanish endecasílabo sonnets or décimas was found. The Tiny Poet's ChatGPT examples are single anecdotes.
- No décima (espinela) generator with published results was found.
- ALBERTI's per-task F1 numbers were not re-read here (see the prior metre note).
- The University of the Basque Country thesis (+18.28% Spanish metre/rhyme accuracy from fine-tuning) remains snippet-only (prior note).

## Italian: Dante/terza rima generation, GePpeTto, endecasillabo constraints

### Takeaway
Measured Italian metrical *generation* rests on one study: Zugarini, Melacci & Maggini (2019). It used a syllable-level LSTM on the Divina Commedia with transfer from 67M syllables of web Italian, then generate-2,000-and-score selection. Experts rated rhyme and style acceptable, metre and emotion weak. I found no peer-reviewed 2023–2026 LLM study of Italian endecasillabo or sonnet generation. The "Galatolo" Dante work named in the brief could not be located.

### Cited Findings
- **Data:** Divina Commedia split into 3,768 training, 471 validation and 472 test tercets (about 180k syllables). Pretraining on PAISÀ (200k documents, about 836k sentences, more than 67M syllables) and on Dante's prose (1,752 sentences, about 157k syllables). — [Zugarini, Melacci, Maggini, "Neural Poetry: Learning to Generate Poems using Syllables", ICANN 2019, LNCS 11730, arXiv:1908.08861](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1908.08861)
- **Model:** syllable-level LSTM with a 1,884-token syllable vocabulary, 300-d embeddings and a 1,024 state. Test perplexity: 12.39 trained on the Commedia alone, 10.55 with PAISÀ → prose → Commedia transfer. — [arXiv:1908.08861](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1908.08861)
- **Selection:** batches of 2,000 candidate tercets are scored. The score penalises a line count other than 3 and every line not equal to 11 syllables, rewards a line-1/line-3 rhyme, gives a bonus for Commedia vocabulary and a strong penalty for out-of-vocabulary words. — [arXiv:1908.08861](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1908.08861)
- **Human evaluation:** 13 non-experts judged Dante's tercets real 64% of the time and the model's 28% (the "56.25% relative difference"). Experts scored on 0–5 (model vs Dante): readability 2.04 vs 4.34, emotion 1.73 vs 3.87, **metre 1.90 vs 4.45**, rhyme 3.37 vs 4.50, style 2.49 vs 4.34. The text says 44 expert judges, the table 4 (inconsistent in the source). The authors propose reinforcement learning as future work. — [arXiv:1908.08861](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1908.08861)
- A follow-up applied the same syllable approach to English blank verse (Wordsworth's *Prelude*). — [Zugarini, Lewis, Alonso, ICCC 2021 (search result)](https://computationalcreativity.net/iccc21/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/ICCC_2021_paper_31.pdf)
- **GePpeTto** (De Mattei, Cafagna, Dell'Orletta, Nissim, Guerini 2020) is the first Italian GPT-2. Its paper evaluates perplexity, linguistic profiling and human sentence completion ("natural more often than not"), with no poetry evaluation. — [arXiv:2004.14253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14253)
- Italian resources: Biblioteca Italiana poetry subset, more than 18,000 works by more than 159 authors (github.com/linhd-postdata/biblioteca_italiana). The survey also cites Corbetta et al. (2024) for Italian LLM *evaluation* (not read). — [Koziev & Sinev 2026](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/20584/27307/55382). Syllabification of the Commedia: Asperti & Dal Bianco, [arXiv:2010.13515](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.13515) (prior note).
- A Hugging Face model card describes a GRPO-trained Dante-style hendecasyllable model on Pleias-350m-Preview, trained on 1,000 Commedia blocks for one epoch (search-engine summary). The card returned HTTP 401 when fetched. Unverified, no evaluation. — [huggingface.co/ruggsea/hf_home_test](https://huggingface.co/ruggsea/hf_home_test)
- CLiC-it 2025 proceedings contain Dante/Cavalcanti parsing and an LLM analysis of Cavalcanti's *Rime*, but no generation paper (search result, not opened). — [ACL Anthology CLiC-it 2025](https://aclanthology.org/events/clicit-2025)

### Inferences
- The one measured Italian system failed on metre even with an explicit 11-syllable penalty. Two reasons fit the data: syllable counting by orthography ignores synalepha and dialefe, and the endecasillabo also needs accents (6th/10th or 4th/8th/10th), which the score did not check. A deterministic Italian checker for PoesIA should score accent position as well as count, or it will repeat this failure.
- Transfer from general Italian prose was worth about 15% perplexity in the 2019 study. Modern multilingual LLMs already hold this, so Italian form is a constraint-and-selection problem rather than a data problem.

### Gaps
- No Italian sonnet (sonetto, ABBA ABBA CDE CDE) generator with measured results, and no LLM metre measurement in Italian.
- "Galatolo et al." Dante generation: not found under author or topic searches. It may not exist as described.
- Italian rhyme and metre gold for evaluating a generator was not investigated beyond the corpora above.

## German: ByGPT5 (Belouadi & Eger), quatrain corpora (QuaTrain, DLK, Haider), diversity, translation

### Takeaway
German has the strongest end-to-end evidence. **ByGPT5** (Belouadi & Eger, ACL 2023) is a token-free decoder (73.5M–289M) fine-tuned on about 1.4M auto-labelled German quatrains. It beat GPT-2, mT5 and ByT5 on rhyme by 5–20%, matched or beat humans on metre in human evaluation, and dominated ChatGPT on rhyme-scheme adherence. Its follow-up (Chen et al., EMNLP 2024) adds that character-level, style-conditioned models are also the most *diverse*. All systems, LLaMA-3-8B included, are under-diverse and mis-distribute rhyme. Rhyme is learned from examples only at scale: the earlier RNN attempt and a 5% data subset both failed.

Naming flag: the brief's "GPoeT (Belouadi & Eger)" conflates two papers. GPoeT is Popescu-Belis et al. 2023 (English, see below). Belouadi & Eger's system is ByGPT5.

### Cited Findings
- **ByGPT5, models:** the ByT5 decoder is re-initialised as a decoder-only model in small (73.5M), base (139.2M) and medium (289.1M) sizes. It is pretrained 50k steps on OpenWebText2 (English) and cc100 (German), and fine-tuned with style codes (rhyme scheme, metre, alliteration level) as a prompt. — [Belouadi & Eger, ACL 2023, arXiv:2212.10474, Table 1, §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.10474)
- **ByGPT5, data:** the labelled corpora are tiny (Anti-K German 4k verses, EPG64 English 1k). Large unlabelled corpora (DLK German 2.8M verses, EPG English 2.8M) were auto-labelled with classifiers. Using real and pseudo-quatrains this gave **QuaTrain**: over 660k English and 1.4M German quatrains. — [arXiv:2212.10474, Table 2, §5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.10474)
- **ByGPT5, automatic results:** evaluated on 2,700 German and 3,600 English generated quatrains. On rhyme, "all ByGPT5 variants collectively outperform all GPT-2 models on both languages by 5%–20%". ByT5 beats mT5 by about 5%, and ByGPT5-small beats ByT5-small by 2–15%. Base beat medium, which the authors suspect is overfitting. On metre "all models perform very similar", since metre is syllable-level and subword models learn it too. English metre scores are lower than German because over 80% of English QuaTrain is iambic (about 60% in German). — [arXiv:2212.10474, §6.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.10474)
- **ByGPT5, human evaluation and analyses:** ByGPT5 ranked best on rhyme, metre and human-likeness and "even surpass[es] human performance in the meter property". Low-resource training on 33k quatrains (5% of English QuaTrain) dropped rhyme adherence by 15–40%, metre by 5–20% and alliteration by 5–10%. The authors conclude that more data helps "only when coupled with architectures that excel at character-level tasks". Extractive memorisation: GPT-2 medium copied over 3% of English outputs; ByGPT5 medium copied 0% in English and 0.81% in German. Against ChatGPT, the rhyme scorer preferred ChatGPT's quatrain in only 15% of pairs, because ChatGPT puts rhymes "at arbitrary positions". — [arXiv:2212.10474, §6.2, §7.1–7.2, §7.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.10474)
- **Earlier failure, Wöckener et al. 2021:** conditioned RNN LMs learned length and sentiment from examples but "do[es] not grasp sound phenomena like alliteration and rhyming". Newer GPT-2 models showed similar rhyming problems. — [LaTeCH-CLfL 2021, aclanthology 2021.latechclfl-1.7](https://aclanthology.org/2021.latechclfl-1.7/)
- **Diversity, Chen, Gröner, Zarrieß, Eger (EMNLP 2024):** German and English. QuaTrain train/dev: DE 253,843/28,205, EN 181,670/20,186. SonNet pseudo-sonnets: DE 72,526, EN 51,905. 36 models: DeepSpeare, Structured Adversary, ByGPT5 140M/290M, GPT-2 117M/774M, GPTNeo 125M/1.3B, LLaMA-2 7B/13B and LLaMA-3 8B, each unconditioned and style-conditioned. Human quatrains are under 15% non-rhyming (ABCD), and ABAB is over 35% of German quatrains. Unconditioned LLMs (except LLaMA-3) produce many ABCD, i.e. they under-rhyme. Rhyme-distribution KL (DE/EN): unconditioned 1.107/0.937, conditioned 0.913/0.785, character-level 0.893/0.895. No model matches human lexical (MTLD DE 283.1, EN 183.4) or semantic diversity. Best average rank: conditioned ByGPT5-L in German (3.1) and conditioned ByGPT5-S in English (4.8). "Style-conditioning and character-level modeling increase diversity across nearly all dimensions." Larger models memorise more. — [arXiv:2406.15267v2 / aclanthology 2024.emnlp-main.1097](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.15267v2)
- **Haider's corpora:** DLK, 2.8M German verses (as used in ByGPT5). The 2024 reference corpus of New High German poetry has about 65k public-domain poems (c. 1600–1920s) annotated with tokens, syllables, POS, metre and verse measure. — [Haider, LREC-COLING 2024, 677–683, aclanthology 2024.lrec-main.59](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.59); metrical tagging gold in [Haider, EACL 2021, arXiv:2102.08858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08858) (prior note); emotion labels Po-Emo (Haider et al., LREC 2020), used by ByGPT5 §7.3.
- **German LLM translation of Shakespeare's sonnets (EN→DE, 154 sonnets):** LLMs trail professional translators, especially on rhyme. "Iteratively refining the result emerges as a successful strategy" for form, but it costs grammaticality and meaning. Abstract only; models and numbers not seen. — [Klumpp, LaTeCH-CLfL 2026, doi:10.18653/v1/2026.latechclfl-1.18](https://aclanthology.org/2026.latechclfl-1.18/)
- **Engineering example (not peer-reviewed):** `llm_poetry_generation` is a German pipeline. A fine-tuned GPT-2-large proposes about 10 candidates per line, which are filtered by a stress model (IPA with stress, about 97% on validation). Lines are shortened or extended with a spaCy tree plus BERT/GPT-2 perplexity, and rhyme endings are chosen with a Siamese LSTM rhyme embedder (97.8% on a cleaned set). Linked to Jörg (2024), "Poetische KI", in *Digitale Hermeneutik* (Hagen University Press). — [github.com/andjoer/llm_poetry_generation](https://github.com/andjoer/llm_poetry_generation)
- **Multi-agent diversity (Zhang & Eger 2024):** non-cooperative multi-agent setups raised diversity by 3.0–3.7 pp and novelty by 5.6–11.3 pp (distinct/novel n-grams) for GPT-2 agents, across 96k generated poems. Prompting-based GPT-3/GPT-4 agents *lost* lexical diversity over time. — [arXiv:2409.03659](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.03659)

### Inferences
- For German, a small byte-level model trained on about 1M quatrains beats subword models on rhyme. An 8B subword LLM, however, has far more semantic capacity. The practical reading for PoesIA is not "switch to bytes" but "don't expect the 8B subword model to place rhymes by itself". The rhyme word should come from the deterministic engine (ChatGPT's 15% shows that even a much larger model misplaces rhymes).
- The 5% data ablation (33k quatrains, −15 to −40% rhyme) indicates the scale needed to *learn* rhyme by fine-tuning: hundreds of thousands of quatrains. PoesIA's curated per-language corpora will be far smaller, which argues for decoding-time control over training-time learning of rhyme.

### Gaps
- No German Sonett (14-line) generator with measured metre was found. ByGPT5 and Chen et al. work on quatrains, and "SonNet" pseudo-sonnets were only used for diversity.
- Per-model numeric rhyme and metre scores in ByGPT5 are only in Figure 3 (a chart). Text values are relative (5–20%).
- No multilingual ByGPT5 exists. The models are separate English and German monolingual fine-tunes.

## French: alexandrine generation, Van de Cruys 2019/2020, interactive constrained systems, corpora

### Takeaway
French poetry generation evidence is pre-LLM. Van de Cruys trained a GRU encoder-decoder on 500M words of French web prose, with a rhyme prior, an NMF topic prior and 2,000-candidate scoring. It gave the best automatic French poems in human evaluation, but still far below human (amateur) poems. Popescu-Belis et al.'s interactive CR-PO approximated syllables by *character counts* (4 chars per syllable, 48 for an alexandrine). I found **no study measuring alexandrine (12-syllable, 6+6 caesura) accuracy for any neural model or LLM**. "Métrique en Ligne" generation work was not found.

### Cited Findings
- **Van de Cruys (ACL 2020; French version TALN 2019):** separate English and French models. Each is trained on 500M words of filtered CommonCrawl sentences (up to 20 words, with function-word filter), 15K vocabulary, 2-layer GRU encoder-decoder with 2,048 hidden units, generating verse right-to-left. A KenLM trigram and a 100-dimension NMF topic model are trained on a 10-billion-word corpus. Around **2,000 candidates per verse** are scored by the harmonic mean of LM log-probability, rhyme compliance, topic fit, syllable count (Gaussian with µ = 12, σ = 2) and n-gram score. Scheme ABAB CDCD, no cherry-picking. — [Van de Cruys 2020, ACL, aclanthology 2020.acl-main.223, §3–4](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.223.pdf); French precursor [TALN 2019, 2019.jeptalnrecital-long.8](https://aclweb.org/anthology/2019.jeptalnrecital-long.8.pdf)
- **Van de Cruys 2020, French human evaluation** (22 native annotators, 30 poems, 1–5 scale; fluency / coherence / meaningfulness / poeticness / % judged human): unconstrained RNN 3.45/2.73/2.59/2.55/27%; +rhyme 3.82/2.55/2.18/3.23/14%; +rhyme+specified topic 3.82/3.82/3.55/3.95/45%; human (amateur poets from short-edition.com) 4.59/4.59/4.50/4.81/95%. Rhyme significantly improved poeticness (p < 0.05). The topic constraint improved coherence and meaningfulness. — [Table 4](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.223.pdf)
- **Same paper, English:** best model 3.64/3.41/3.27/3.86 with 55% judged human, vs human 3.73/3.73/3.68/4.00 (73%). Deep-speare 2.11/2.00/2.00/3.00 and Hafez 3.44/3.11/3.11/3.50. No comparable French system existed ("no other readily available systems exist for French"). — [Table 4, §4.2](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.223.pdf)
- **CR-PO, interactive constrained generation (Popescu-Belis et al., LREC 2022):** the authors state that LMs "cannot learn such constraints from the data, which is scarce … even for a well-resourced language such as French". About 14.45 MB of French poems were used (two collections of about 7.7 MB each, under 4% overlap), with CamemBERT adaptation and a character-level RNN. Line length is controlled by approximating 4 characters per syllable: for a 12-syllable line, 48 characters, generating 85% (41 characters) and then allowing a 2–13-character ending. Rhyme comes from a phonetic dictionary. Evaluation consisted of public exhibitions and log analysis, with no metre accuracy reported. — [aclanthology 2022.lrec-1.377](https://aclanthology.org/2022.lrec-1.377.pdf)
- **Modern French free verse, Hämäläinen, Alnajjar & Poibeau (ICCC 2022):** RoBERTa encoder plus BelGPT-2 decoder. Human 5-point scores ranged from 3.57 (typicality, emotionality) to 3.79 (understandability). Not metrical. — [arXiv:2212.02911](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.02911)
- **French resources:** HF `manu/french_poetry` has 1,821 poems with metadata ([Koziev & Sinev 2026](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/20584/27307/55382)). Neural rhyme detection for English, German and French (Haider & Kuhn 2018), cited in the same survey. Rhyme discovery in Czech, English and French (Plecháč 2018) is in the prior note. PoeTree treebanks include French (Plecháč et al. 2024).

### Inferences
- French metre is especially poorly suited to orthographic or character proxies: the e muet/e caduc counts before a consonant and elides before a vowel and at line end, plus diérèse/synérèse and the 6+6 caesura. CR-PO's 4-chars-per-syllable heuristic shows the field has not solved this generatively. PoesIA's French alexandrine engine must do real syllabification with e-caduc rules, and there is no published generator to benchmark against.
- Van de Cruys's French result (rhyme lowers meaningfulness from 2.59 to 2.18 unless topic is also controlled) is a concrete French data point for the form-versus-meaning trade-off. Pair every rhyme constraint with a content or theme constraint.

### Gaps
- No LLM (GPT-4-class or open) alexandrine or French sonnet metre measurement found. No French counterpart of PoeLM or ByGPT5 was found.
- "Métrique en Ligne" / Anamètre-based generation or evaluation of generators: not found in the time available. Its value as scansion gold was not researched here.

## English: Deep-speare, Zest (Tian & Peng 2022), GPoeT, Chakrabarty, Porter & Machery 2024, Walsh 2024, POEMetric 2026

### Takeaway
English has the most systems and the clearest pattern. Constrained decoding (Hafez, Zest) gets near-perfect rhyme and about 75% metre, while few-shot GPT-3 got 14% rhyme and 18% metre. Modern chat LLMs rhyme by default (about 90% of GPT poems contain rhyme) but are stylistically uniform: quatrains, iambs and a small set of words. Lay readers prefer simple ChatGPT poems to canonical poets (Porter & Machery). Expert-oriented 2026 evaluation still puts the best LLM well below humans overall (3.20 vs 4.22).

### Cited Findings
- **Zest, zero-shot sonnets (Tian & Peng, NAACL 2022):** no poetry training data. A T5 content planner is trained on 4,500 CNN summaries and 16,000 r/shortscarystories, and GPT-Neo-2.7B decodes right-to-left. Rhyme words are chosen from CMUdict. Metre is enforced by keeping only beam tokens consistent with iambic pentameter (or reversed first foot or feminine ending). Automatic results (rhyme / metre / 10–11 syllables): Hafez 98.3/76.8/95.7%; few-shot GPT-3 14.0/17.6/30.9%; Prosaic 100/10.1/19.0%; **Zest 100/75.6/98.4%**; human sonnets 94.6/70.7/81.8%. Expert ratings (1–5) for Zest vs human: coherence 3.52 vs 3.82, originality 3.41 vs 3.54, poeticness 3.66 vs 3.68, emotion 3.22 vs 3.56; judged human-written 62.0% vs 83.3%. Constrained decoding "sometimes drops conjunctions and auxiliary verbs". — [aclanthology 2022.naacl-main.262 / arXiv:2205.01821, Tables 2–3, §6](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.01821)
- **GPoeT (Popescu-Belis et al., LaTeCH-CLfL 2023):** GPT-2 124M fine-tuned on the Gutenberg Poetry Corpus (3,085,063 lines, 142 MB) produced rhymes in only about 11% of lines (4.3% perfect, 6.6% assonant). Further fine-tuning on **6 MB (160k lines) of rule-generated synthetic rhyming quatrains** raised this to about 60% for AABB and 45% for ABAB. The rhyme metric (CMU-based) found 80.8% of annotated rhymes in the Chicago Rhyming Poetry Corpus with 9.2% false positives (F1 = 0.61). — [aclanthology 2023.latechclfl-1.2](https://aclanthology.org/2023.latechclfl-1.2.pdf)
- **Deep-speare (Lau et al., ACL 2018):** joint LM + pentameter + rhyme model. Crowd workers could barely tell it from human sonnets. Experts rated it lower on readability and emotion (details in the prior note). Van de Cruys's 2020 English evaluation put Deep-speare at 2.11 fluency and indistinguishable from a random-sentence baseline. — [arXiv:1807.03491](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03491); [Van de Cruys 2020, Table 4](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.223.pdf)
- **Porter & Machery (Scientific Reports 14:26133, 2024):** experiment 1 had 1,634 participants, each seeing five poems by ten canonical poets (Shakespeare, Byron, Dickinson, Eliot, …) and five ChatGPT-3.5 poems "in the style of" those poets. Identification accuracy was 46.6%, below chance, and AI poems were more often judged human than human poems. Experiment 2 had 696 participants rating 14 qualities, and AI poems were rated higher on rhythm and beauty. The authors' explanation: readers misread human complexity as AI incoherence. Critique: Davis, "ChatGPT's poetry is incompetent and banal". — [doi:10.1038/s41598-024-76900-1, PMC11564748](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11564748); [Davis critique](https://cs.nyu.edu/~davise/papers/GPT-Poetry.pdf) (search summaries; full text not re-read here)
- **Walsh, Preus & Gronski, "Does ChatGPT have a poetic style?" (CHR 2024):** 5.7k GPT-3.5/GPT-4 poems across 24 forms vs 3.7k Poetry Foundation / Academy of American Poets poems. Poems with at least one rhyme: human 65.0%, GPT-3.5 90.2%, GPT-4 89.5%. Mean share of rhymed lines: 29.45% vs 63.87% / 65.20%. Poems with at least one quatrain: 18.4% vs 70.4% / 63.3%. Dominant iambic metre (hand-annotated sample): human just under 40%, GPT-3.5 about 74%, GPT-4 about 53%. Limericks: median 25 lines (should be 5). Sonnets: median 14 lines. GPT-3.5 uses "embrace", "grace", "dance" or "dreams" in 87% of poems; GPT-4 uses "echo" or "whisper" in 75%. — [arXiv:2410.15299, Tables 3, 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.15299); CEUR-WS Vol-3834 paper 122
- **Form recognition vs production:** GPT-4/4o identify fixed forms well (Walsh, Antoniak & Preus, Findings EMNLP 2024), as covered in the prior note. — [aclanthology 2024.findings-emnlp.914](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.914.pdf)
- **POEMetric (Li, Wang, Wilkinson; ICLR 2026):** 203 human poems in 7 fixed English forms vs 6,090 poems from 30 LLMs. The best model reached form accuracy 4.26/5 (Gemini-2.5-Pro judge) and theme alignment 4.99. Overall quality: humans 4.22 vs best LLM 3.20. "All models failed to reach the same level of advanced abilities as human poets" (creativity, idiosyncrasy, emotional resonance, imagery, literary devices). Abstract only; per-model and per-form breakdowns not read. — [arXiv:2604.03695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03695)
- **Instruction-tuned collaboration, CoPoet (Chakrabarty, Padmakumar & He, EMNLP 2022):** a T5 fine-tuned on poetry-writing instructions was "competitive with" InstructGPT. In a study with 15 crowdworkers, third-party evaluators preferred collaboratively written poems (no numbers on the abstract page). — [arXiv:2210.13669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13669)
- **Chakrabarty et al., "Art or Artifice?" (CHI 2024)** is about short *stories*, not poems: LLM stories passed 3–10× fewer Torrance-style expert tests than professional stories, and no LLM judge correlated positively with experts. Relevant only as evidence that LLM-as-judge is unreliable for creative quality. — [arXiv:2309.14556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14556v2)
- **Zero-shot metre (Agirrezabal & Gonçalo Oliveira 2024; Llama-2 7B/70B, English):** see prior note (per-line syllable accuracy 0.8–26.5%). **PhonologyBench:** about 17% human–LLM gap on rhyme-word generation and about 45% on syllable counting (search summary of [arXiv:2404.02456](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.02456v1)).

### Inferences
- English shows that "good form" and "good poem" diverge. Zest reaches human-level poeticness with constrained decoding, yet still loses on coherence and on being judged human (62% vs 83%). Walsh shows LLMs over-produce rhymed iambic quatrains with a small vocabulary. For PoesIA, whose owner's taste is modernismo/romanticism, that default may *look* close to the target form, but its lexical tics ("whisper", "embrace", "echo" and their Spanish equivalents) are the quality risk to screen for.
- Porter & Machery measure lay preference for accessible verse, not expert judgement. They should not be read as evidence that LLM poems are good by the owner's criteria.

### Gaps
- POEMetric per-form metre and rhyme numbers and the model ranking were not extracted (abstract only).
- Lau et al. 2018's numeric crowd and expert results were not re-read.

## Multilingual poetry models and cross-lingual transfer

### Takeaway
There is **no multilingual poetry *generation* model or benchmark covering es/it/de/fr/en with metre and rhyme**. The multilingual items found are an analysis model (ALBERTI, 12 languages), corpora (PULPO, PoeTree), bilingual EN+DE studies built as two monolingual models (ByGPT5, Chen et al.), and single-language studies in other languages (Russian, Czech, Greek). The tokenisation results from those languages transfer to PoesIA's design. The 2026 JAIR survey itself calls multilingual generative poetry evaluation an open need.

### Cited Findings
- **Survey position:** Koziev & Sinev (JAIR 86, 2026; 2017–2025 literature) note that "language choice is often constrained by corpus availability". They cite PoeTree (Czech, English, French, German, Hungarian, Italian, Portuguese, Russian, Spanish treebanks) as a multilingual resource and identify "multilingual generative poetry evaluation" as a gap. Instruction-tuned prompting "has become the dominant method for controlling poetry generation". — [doi:10.1613/jair.1.20584](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/20584/27307/55382)
- **ALBERTI / PULPO (12 languages):** analysis only (see Spanish section). — [arXiv:2307.01387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01387)
- **Czech, tokenisation and per-line hints (Chudoba & Rosa 2024, "GPT Czech Poet"):** Corpus of Czech Verse with 2,310,917 verses and 374,537 strophes. Czech GPT-2 variants of 86–137M with four tokenisers. Adding **per-verse parameters (syllable count + ending hint) to the training format raised rhyme-scheme accuracy from 49.6% to 89.8%**; metre stayed at about 94%. Final models (number of syllables / ending accuracy / rhyme / metre): BPE base 91.8/94.6/86.5/94.5%; syllable tokeniser 94.4/95.2/88.7/94.6%; character (Unicode) tokeniser 91.1/91.2/73.8/93.5% with plain decoding, but **97.8/98.0/94.0/94.0% with "forced generation"**. Forced generation means re-inserting the verse parameters before each line at inference. A BPE tokeniser trained only on 2 GB of poetry was worst. Accuracies are measured by learned validators: rhyme validator 96.96%, syllable-based metre validator 89.90%. — [arXiv:2407.12790, Tables 2–3, 6–7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12790)
- **Russian, character vs syllable vs BPE vs byte (Koziev & Fenogenova, LaTeCH-CLfL 2025):** fine-tuning set of 1,704,418 samples. On 1,000 quatrain prompts (technicality / rhyming level, i.e. share of ABAB quatrains): syllable-tokenised stGPT 100M 0.70/0.472 and 350M 0.72/0.467; character-level CharLLaMa 1.3B 0.58/0.352 and 2.6B 0.59/0.339; fine-tuned Mistral-7B (BPE) 0.57/0.192; FRED-T5-1.7B 0.26/0.126; ruGPT3-large 0.06/0.002; **ByT5-large 0.02/0.001**; human 0.81/0.683. For sonnets, stGPT exceeded humans on technicality (0.712 vs 0.555), but syllable models "often produce texts with grammatical and fluency issues". In expert Bradley–Terry ratings: human 1.49, CharLLaMa-1.3B 0.23, CharMamba-1.3B −0.20. — [doi:10.18653/v1/2025.latechclfl-1.6, Tables 3–5](https://aclanthology.org/2025.latechclfl-1.6.pdf)
- **Greek generate–verify–refine:** pure LLM under 4% valid rhyming poems → 73.1% with a symbolic checker loop (prior note). — [aclanthology 2026.latechclfl-1.9](https://aclanthology.org/2026.latechclfl-1.9.pdf)
- **Bilingual EN/DE studies** (ByGPT5; Chen et al. 2024) train separate models per language. No cross-lingual transfer experiment was run. — [arXiv:2212.10474](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.10474); [arXiv:2406.15267](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.15267v2)
- Earlier multilingual work: Tikhonov & Yamshchikov (2018) "Guess who? Multilingual approach for the automated generation of author-stylized poetry" (English and Russian); Song et al. (2023) "Towards zero-shot multilingual poetry translation". Both are cited by the survey and were not read. — [Koziev & Sinev 2026](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/20584/27307/55382)
- **Multilingual Llama/mT5/mBART poetry:** the survey lists Boggia et al. (2022), mBART fine-tuned on English Gutenberg poetry line by line (English only). No mT5/mBART *multilingual* metrical poetry result was found.

### Inferences
- The Czech result is the most transferable finding for PoesIA. Explicit per-line targets (syllable count + rhyme ending) in the prompt or format nearly double rhyme accuracy, and repeating them before each line ("forced generation") adds more. PoesIA's engine already computes these per line, so it can write them into the prompt line by line.
- Byte-level is not uniformly better. ByGPT5 (Latin script, decoder-only) wins, but ByT5 collapsed in Russian (multi-byte Cyrillic, encoder-decoder). Czech character-level models needed forced generation to win. For Latin-script es/it/de/fr/en the byte/character advantage is plausible but small next to explicit control.

### Gaps
- No benchmark measures one model's metre and rhyme across es/it/de/fr/en. Apertus's own technical report was not examined for poetry or phonology results (out of scope here).
- No study of cross-lingual transfer of metre/rhyme skill, e.g. whether fine-tuning on German sonnets helps Italian, was found.

## What the results imply for a small multilingual model (Apertus 8B + deterministic engine)

### Takeaway
Across languages, form correctness came from **explicit per-line targets plus an external checker and selection or repair**, not from model size or fine-tuning alone. Fine-tuning learns rhyme only with hundreds of thousands of examples, plus character-level models or explicit per-line hints. Hard token-level constraints secure form but measurably cost fluency and meaning. Character/byte models help rhyme in Latin scripts, but an 8B subword LLM with a rhyme-word-first engine gets the benefit without changing tokeniser.

### Cited Findings
- **Data sizes behind systems that learned form:** ByGPT5 used about 1.4M (DE) and 660k (EN) quatrains, and 33k quatrains cost 15–40% rhyme adherence ([arXiv:2212.10474 §7.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.10474)). Czech GPT Czech Poet used 2.3M verses ([arXiv:2407.12790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12790)). Russian character/syllable models used 1.7M fine-tuning samples ([2025.latechclfl-1.6](https://aclanthology.org/2025.latechclfl-1.6.pdf)). GPoeT gained rhyme from only 6 MB of *synthetic* rhyming quatrains (11% → about 60% AABB, 45% ABAB) ([2023.latechclfl-1.2](https://aclanthology.org/2023.latechclfl-1.2.pdf)). Systems that did *not* learn form from poems relied on decoding: PoeLM used 700 GB of prose ([arXiv:2205.12206](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.12206)), Van de Cruys 500M words of prose ([2020.acl-main.223](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.223.pdf)), Zest news and stories ([arXiv:2205.01821](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.01821)). Small poem sets: The Tiny Poet's 5,262 Spanish poems needed backtracking constraints ([doi:10.26342/2025-74-22](http://journal.sepln.org/sepln/ojs/ojs/index.php/pln/article/download/6684/4075)), and Zugarini's 3,768 tercets gave weak metre ([arXiv:1908.08861](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1908.08861)).
- **Constraints vs fine-tuning, head to head where available:** few-shot GPT-3 14% rhyme / 17.6% metre vs constrained Zest 100% / 75.6% ([Tian & Peng 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.01821)). ChatGPT vs fine-tuned ByGPT5 on rhyme scheme: ByGPT5 preferred in 85% of pairs ([ByGPT5 §7.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.10474)). Czech per-line hints raised rhyme 49.6 → 89.8% ([2407.12790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12790)). Greek verify-refine loop: under 4% → 73.1% (prior note). Fine-tuned Mistral-7B on Russian poetry reached a rhyming level of only 0.192 vs human 0.683 ([2025.latechclfl-1.6](https://aclanthology.org/2025.latechclfl-1.6.pdf)).
- **Costs of hard constraints:** Zest's constrained decoding drops function words ([§6](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.01821)). Van de Cruys found that a "blind modification of the output … is detrimental to syntactic fluency", so the prior is applied only when entropy is high ([§3.2](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.223.pdf)). French rhyme-only control lowered meaningfulness 2.59 → 2.18 ([Table 4](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.223.pdf)). Iterative form refinement in EN→DE sonnet translation costs grammaticality and meaning ([Klumpp 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.latechclfl-1.18/)). Russian syllable models beat humans on technicality but write ungrammatically ([2025.latechclfl-1.6](https://aclanthology.org/2025.latechclfl-1.6.pdf)).
- **Byte/character level:** ByGPT5 beats GPT-2 on rhyme by 5–20% and smaller beats medium ([arXiv:2212.10474](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.10474)). Character models are the most diverse ([Chen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.15267v2)). The Czech character model is best only with forced per-line parameters ([2407.12790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12790)). ByT5 fails in Russian ([2025.latechclfl-1.6](https://aclanthology.org/2025.latechclfl-1.6.pdf)).
- **Diversity:** all systems, LLaMA-3-8B included, are under-diverse, and conditioning on style tokens increases diversity ([Chen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.15267v2)). Chat LLMs have strong lexical tics ([Walsh et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.15299)).

### Inferences
- **Recommended order for PoesIA (synthesis):**
  1. The engine fixes per-line targets (syllable count, stress pattern, rhyme ending or rhyme word) and writes them into the prompt line by line (the Czech "forced generation" and PoeLM descriptors, prompt-level rather than decoding-level).
  2. Sample several candidates per line or stanza.
  3. Gate deterministically.
  4. Repair only failing lines with the exact error.
  5. Rank survivors by meaning and fluency, not by form score (PoeLM's unconditioned rerank; Van de Cruys's harmonic-mean scorer with a topic term).
- **Fine-tuning Apertus 8B:** use it for *style and register* (modernismo diction, imagery, avoiding chat-LLM tics), where the evidence shows the gaps are (coherence, imagery, emotion). Do not use it to teach rhyme placement. If form is trained at all, include per-line target annotations in the training format: the Czech ablation shows this is what makes rhyme learnable. GPoeT shows synthetic rhymed data can stand in for scarce real data, but it adds rhymes, not quality.
- **Tokeniser:** don't switch away from a subword LLM for rhyme alone. The byte-level gains (5–20%) are smaller than the gains from explicit per-line targets plus checking (tens of points), and an 8B model's semantic capacity is what the evidence says is missing. Whether Apertus's tokenizer splits Spanish/Italian/French word endings finely enough to make rhyme-ending conditioning easy is worth a quick empirical check (Apertus tokenizer not examined here).
- **Per-language risk ranking (synthesis):** French alexandrine has the least published support (no neural metre measurement; e-caduc rules). Italian endecasillabo is next (one 2019 study, metre judged weak; accents needed). Spanish has two constrained systems but no LLM metre study. German and English quatrains are best covered, but sonnet-length metre is measured only in English (Zest about 76% iambic lines).

### Gaps
- No study fine-tunes an open 7–8B multilingual LLM on metrical poetry in several of es/it/de/fr/en and reports metre/rhyme per language.
- No published comparison of decoding-time constraints vs fine-tuning on the *same* model and language with human quality ratings. The comparisons above mix models and languages.
- RL with rule rewards (GRPO-style) for European metrical poetry: only an unverified Italian HF card. The analogues are Chinese (Tang poetry, Songci), not read here.
